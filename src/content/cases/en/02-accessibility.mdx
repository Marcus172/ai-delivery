## Thousands of marks, eighteen defects

The audit matrix covered every page in two languages and two themes, and marked a criterion as failing per page. Nothing pointed at an element. Before fixing anything I had the matrix analysed as data: most failing criteria failed on almost every page, which means the defect was in a shared component, not on the page. The strategy followed from that: fix the component, and the test that proves it fixed, instead of working through the marks one by one.

## A manifest with a state machine

Each systemic criterion and each remaining page-level finding lives in a machine-readable manifest with a schema and a lifecycle: pending, in progress, test written, passing, done, with blocked and won't-fix carrying a reason. The states are not decoration: *passing* means an automated test is green, *done* means a person confirmed it with a screen reader. Three agents work on the manifest, one that turns a new audit matrix into findings, one that writes the regression test for a finding next to the code, one that runs the vision audit, and a handful of commands print status, propose the next item, export the state for management and absorb a re-scoped audit without a reset. When the client's analyst widened the scope in September, the manifest took the new rows in without starting over.

## Six layers, one of them human

<TestPyramid locale={locale} />

<Stat id="a11y-specs" locale={locale} />

Automated checks catch perhaps a third of real barriers, and the documentation says so. Six of the systemic criteria are purely visual, focus rings, contrast in context, reflow, which is why the fifth layer exists: a browser agent walks the screens and a vision model reads the screenshots against those criteria, with calibrated examples, and a person reviews what it flags. The sixth layer is the only path to *done*: a person with a screen reader. No agent moves a finding there.

The fix landed in the shared button, the component spec asserts a visible outline on focus, the journey test tabs to the button for real, and a person confirmed it with a screen reader before the finding moved to done.</p>

  <strong>The honest part.</strong> Three systemic criteria are blocked by decisions outside this codebase and are listed as such, with the reason, in a document that separates what we fix from what another supplier owns or the client has yet to decide.
